HR Employee Attrition Analysis — Power BI
📊 Project Overview

This project analyzes employee attrition using Microsoft Power BI. The objective is to understand who is leaving the organization and which factors are associated with employee turnover.

The dashboard provides an interactive view of employee demographics, departments, job roles, satisfaction levels, overtime, income, and tenure to help identify potential retention challenges.

🎯 Business Objectives
Understand the overall employee attrition rate.
Identify departments and job roles with higher attrition.
Analyze attrition across different age groups.
Examine the relationship between overtime and employee attrition.
Analyze the impact of job satisfaction and work-life balance.
Compare attrition across different income groups.
Identify patterns related to employee tenure.

🔍 Key Insights
Overall employee attrition is approximately 16.12%.
Research & Development has the highest number of employee exits.
Sales has the highest departmental attrition rate.
Sales Representatives show the highest attrition rate among job roles.
Employees working overtime have a significantly higher attrition rate (30.5%) compared with employees not working overtime (10.4%).
Lower job satisfaction is associated with higher employee attrition.
Lower work-life balance is associated with higher employee attrition.
Employees earning below 3K per month have the highest attrition rate among the income groups analyzed.
Employees aged 29 and 31 have the highest number of exits, with 18 exits each.
📈 Business Takeaways

The analysis suggests that employee retention efforts could focus on areas such as:

Reviewing overtime workload.
Improving employee job satisfaction.
Supporting better work-life balance.
Reviewing compensation for lower-income employees.
Investigating retention challenges within Sales and specific high-attrition job roles.

These findings represent associations within the dataset and should not be interpreted as proof of direct causation.
